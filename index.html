Fabric Guide: Lawn, Cambric, Khaddar, Chiffon and Velvet Explained

Ask any experienced Pakistani shopper what makes a good outfit and they will rarely start with the print. They will start with the fabric. In a country where summer temperatures cross forty degrees and winter evenings in the north can drop below freezing, fabric is not a detail — it is the decision everything else hangs on.

Yet the labels can be confusing. Lawn, cambric, khaddar, chiffon, velvet — shops stack them side by side, prices vary wildly, and two suits that look identical on the shelf can feel completely different after one wash. This guide explains the five fabrics every Pakistani wardrobe runs on: what each one is, when to wear it, how to recognise it, and how to look after it.

Why Fabric Knowledge Matters More in Pakistan

Our clothing does more work than in milder climates. A summer outfit must breathe through load-shedding afternoons and humid evenings; a winter suit must insulate through fog season. Fabric also decides how an outfit drapes, how long it lasts, how it survives the washing machine, and — very often — whether that "bargain" was actually a bargain. Once you can identify fabrics confidently, you also become much harder to fool, which is why this knowledge pairs well with learning how to check original branded clothes against copies.

Lawn: The Summer King

Lawn is a fine, lightweight cotton with a smooth, slightly crisp finish. It is the fabric of Pakistani summers — printed three-piece suits, everyday kurtas and dupattas from March to September.

What to look for: Good lawn feels soft but not slippery, holds a print crisply, and is woven tightly enough that you cannot easily see through it when you hold it to the light. Premium lawn has a higher thread count, which is why it survives a full season of weekly washing while cheaper versions fade and thin out.

Best for: Daily wear, office, university — anywhere you need to stay cool. For more on building outfits around it in the hottest months, see these ideas for staying stylish while beating the summer heat in Pakistan.

Care: Machine wash cold, dry in shade to protect prints, and iron on a medium setting.

Cambric: The Mid-Season Bridge

Cambric is also cotton, but woven a little denser and heavier than lawn, with a smooth, almost silky face. Think of it as lawn's more polished sibling — the fabric for the in-between months of spring and autumn, and for people who find lawn too flimsy.

What to look for: Cambric should feel substantial without being stiff. It takes embroidery beautifully, which is why so many mid-season embroidered shirts are cambric. Solid-coloured cambric trousers are a wardrobe staple for the same reason.

Best for: Office wear, semi-formal day events, and the weeks when the weather cannot decide what it is doing.

Care: Similar to lawn, though cambric wrinkles a little more — iron while slightly damp for the crispest finish.

Khaddar: The Winter Workhorse

Khaddar is a handloom-style woven cotton, recognisable by its slightly textured, matte surface. Where lawn is air, khaddar is earth: warm, durable, and made for December and January.

What to look for: Weight is everything. Heavier khaddar suits the cold of Lahore, Islamabad and the north; lighter weaves are enough for Karachi's short winter. Rub the fabric between your fingers — good khaddar feels dense and even, without thin patches. It also holds embroidery well, so winter designs often pair khaddar shirts with embroidered borders or necklines.

Best for: Everyday winter suits, office wear in the cold months, and anyone who wants warmth without the price of wool.

Care: Wash separately the first few times (deep winter colours can bleed), and store clean — khaddar's natural fibres attract moths in long storage.

Chiffon: The Graceful Formal

Chiffon is a sheer, lightweight woven fabric — traditionally silk, now usually polyester or blended — loved for its fluid drape. In Pakistan it lives mostly in dupattas, formal shirts and evening wear.

What to look for: Chiffon should flow, not cling with static. Run your hand along it: quality chiffon feels smooth with a slight grain, and printed designs should be sharp rather than blurred. Because it is delicate, check hems and edges — chiffon frays, and good brands finish it carefully.

Best for: Festive and formal outfits, dupattas that need to float, and layered looks over a camisole or slip. If you are planning outfits for the festive season, this family Eid shopping preparation guide shows where chiffon pieces fit into a complete wardrobe plan.

Care: Hand wash or use a gentle cycle in a laundry bag, never wring it, and store it rolled or folded away from sharp embellishments that can snag it.

Velvet: Winter's Luxury

Velvet is a woven fabric with a short, dense pile that catches the light — the unmistakable fabric of Pakistani winter formals and wedding season. Traditional velvet was silk; most modern versions are polyester or blended, which makes them more affordable and easier to maintain.

What to look for: The pile should be even, with no bald patches, and the colour should shift richly as you tilt the fabric — that light-play is velvet's signature. Press a fold into it: quality velvet springs back rather than keeping a hard crease.

Best for: Evening wear, mehndis and shaadis, and statement shawls. A single velvet shirt can carry an entire winter festive wardrobe.

Care: Dry-clean for heavily embellished pieces, store hanging or loosely folded with tissue, and never iron directly on the pile — steam from the reverse side instead.

Two More Fabrics Worth Knowing

Karandi sits between cambric and khaddar: a lightly textured woven cotton with more body than cambric and more polish than khaddar. It is a favourite for winter office wear and lightly embroidered suits, and it bridges the awkward weeks when khaddar feels too heavy but cambric feels too light.

Cotton satin — often sold simply as satin-finish cotton — has a smooth, slightly lustrous face that makes solid colours look rich. It appears in trousers, winter shirts and co-ord sets, and is a good choice when you want a dressier look without chiffon's fragility or velvet's weight. Treat it gently in the wash: that sheen is a finish, and harsh washing dulls it. Two more quick additions to your mental list: karandi for mid-weight polish, and cotton satin when a solid needs to look expensive.

Buying Unstitched Fabric With Confidence

Unstitched suits are where fabric knowledge pays for itself, because you are judging the raw material with no stitching to hide behind. Check the fabric length for each piece, confirm which components are included (shirt only, or trouser and dupatta too), and hold every piece to the light. A Quick Reference to Keep

  • Lawn — lightest cotton; peak summer; prints and daily wear.
  • Cambric — denser cotton; spring/autumn; office and embroidery.
  • Khaddar — textured woven cotton; winter; everyday warmth.
  • Chiffon — sheer and flowing; formals and dupattas; handle gently.
  • Velvet — plush pile; winter evenings and weddings; store with care.

Final Word

Prints change every season, but these five fabrics are the permanent grammar of Pakistani dressing. Learn to recognise each one by touch and drape, match it to your weather and your occasion, and you will buy better clothes at every budget — and enjoy wearing them for far longer.
